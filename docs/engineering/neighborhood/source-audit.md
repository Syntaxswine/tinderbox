All prefab roots keep unit scale and use rigid quarter-turn placement. The 1m doors cannot accommodate the unchanged .56m-radius bear and remain closed. This is accepted exterior-only behavior, not a scale conflict.

The selected first-floor/foundation unions are rectangles. The Hopkins meshes actually include a full rectangular low plinth; Ashland's ground basement spans its rectangular plan. Ground mass therefore uses those source dimensions plus .09m for the artist's .18m slab rim, covering the .08m wall half-thickness. Separate porch and stair footprints follow the artist's recipe. Stair treads are 1.16m wide; the .055m rails at +/- .61m extend the footprint to 1.275m, with .0275m end allowance. Porches/steps are blocked as coarse exterior masses; climbing stairs and walking under elevated decks are not supported. Roof eaves, gambrels, chimneys and upper ornament do not fill the ground plane. House gaps are 3m before .09m edge allowances, leaving 1.70m of centre-path clearance after the bear diameter.

All new building fuel is dry wood in the existing finite ground field. One house retains all its colliders until every intersecting footprint cell's surface and deposited fuel is spent, then the house and collider group disappear together. A char tint follows consumed surface fuel. This intentionally approximates an entire building by a ground fuel footprint; it is not wall-climb, interior fire or structural collapse.

Source URLs and interpretation notes remain inside the unmodified records. Generated-art prompts/hashes are preserved. Existing donor license/provenance records remain intact; provenance alone does not establish additional rights. ## View contract and independent review

`snapshot.world.buildings` contains detached, validated descriptors with instance/prefab IDs, rigid placement, native scale, source identity, closed-door metadata, footprint/collider IDs, `burnedFraction` and `destroyed`. `world.camera` carries the neighborhood-only follow profile. Existing scenarios keep their overview. Runtime presentation passes the actual camera and canvas dimensions to the art module. The inherited HUD leaves canvases of 1280 x 494 and 390 x 420 inside the requested 1280 x 800 and 390 x 844 frames. The follow spans account for this reservation; CPU projection places the unchanged 1.67m bear at approximately 49 and 50 pixels tall respectively, before manual zoom.

The architect explicitly allowed a bounded presentation fade for occlusion. Rays toward bear foot/chest/head, the native muzzle and nearby fresh-trail cells intersect conservative bounds computed from the actual original source meshes. A potentially occluding house uses .18 opacity, disables depth writes and shadow casting, and restores the original properties when clear. Source geometry, UVs, scale, fuel and collision never change. This deliberately favors readability over exact silhouette occlusion; a bounding box can fade a house even where a roof has empty space. Transparency appearance still needs a real GPU review.

New `art.diagnostics().housing` fields are `active`, `builds`, `instances`, `models`, `occludingBuildingIds`, `occlusionMethod`, `projection` and `disposed`. Each model reports its source prefab `id`, instance `instanceId`, native scale, rigid position/rotation, `opacityScale`, source mesh/triangle counts, source world bounds and assembler resource counts. Projection reports player NDC, bear height in actual canvas pixels and sample count. Runtime metrics expose `camera.target`, `camera.span`, `camera.zoom` and `camera.followPlayer`. The explicit review control is `workbenches/art/neighborhood.html`, using a production game/UI iframe; the architect-owned integration workbench is untouched. `tools/review-neighborhood-offline.mjs --write` reproduces `offline-review.json`: 623 / 798 / 714 source meshes and 8,867 / 10,637 / 9,306 triangles respectively, twelve projection/occlusion samples, and the review's accelerated six-second burnout fixture. These counts include retained interior source geometry; they are not GPU draw-call or frame-rate measurements.

The browser recheck still fails at WebGL startup before the assets load. See `browser-review.json` and its two screenshots. The task preview and browser tab were retired after that attempt; no successful GPU rendering, visual acceptance, integration promotion or publication is claimed.
