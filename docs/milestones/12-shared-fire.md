# Shared fire rules and night lighting

The regular townhouses currently burn in a flat ground field. Their art projects those fires near the base of each façade. The Ashland pair has elevated heat sites and a separate light setup. Starting from `080634a0e83f8da266897d60e951b680261908fe`, both routes will use shared combustion rules and one street firelight system.

The coder owns `codex/shared-structural-fire-coder` in the existing `coding-9200` worktree. The artist owns `codex/shared-firelight-art` in `art-assets`. The architect reviews the combined result on `codex/review-shared-fire` and promotes it after playtesting. The worker branches preserve their published asset and reuse pins.

Fuel controls ignition heat, available burn time and source output. Surfaces declare native position, orientation, material, connections and support. Heat crosses connected faces over world time, with a bias toward higher fuel and a response to wind where supplied. An adjacent source can go out before the receiver catches, and the receiver cools from its stored heat. Masonry and party walls block heat. A brick façade can show burning timber at openings and eaves. Matches still strike a single reachable tile or surface. Apply the same rules at actual tool, ember and falling fuel contacts. Live supports own local clearance and collision. Preserve the accepted independent 30-second Ashland burns and wind versus calm behavior.

`snapshot.structureFire` is an array of detached world-space samples for active or warming structure sites. Each entry supplies `id`, `buildingId`, `part` (`walls`, `roof` or `ground` for floors), `x`, `y`, `z`, `nx`, `ny`, `nz`, `area`, `exposed`, `flame`, `smoke`, `heatFraction` and `ignitionTime`. Y is height, area is in square metres and ignition time uses the world clock. The renderer takes normal ground fires from `snapshot.cells`. The Ashland frame continues to carry masks, openings, debris and embers. Ordinary façade and roof effects and shared firelight use the surface samples directly.

Review at the normal night zoom with a fixed camera and exposure. A single match should produce a small visible pool on nearby surfaces. As a house becomes involved, the larger fire should light more of the street and the facing house with the architecture still readable. Check that smoke, cold fuel and extinguished sites contribute zero light, and that the light count is bounded as the number of fires grows. On the regular street, follow a real strike on Monroe and on a Hopkins house from the low wall past upper fuel and the eave to sustained roof flame, then confirm local clearance.
