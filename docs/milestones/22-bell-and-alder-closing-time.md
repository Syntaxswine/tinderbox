## The experience

Warm shop windows, a few fair ribbons, footsteps upstairs and an officer passing a street lamp establish an ordinary evening. The player opens Bell & Alder, takes its actual display lantern, explores the shop/alley and starts one concealed fire. A resident notices a visible smoke signal before seeing flame, looks out, and seeks a safe exit. Firelight reaches the street and opposite frontage. The officer remembers the lantern and becomes suspicious; a separate short control shows the stronger response to directly witnessed ignition. Local damage and burning debris change the familiar place.

The main character of this scene is **the developing fire and the place it changes**. Use the owner's Volaire direction: deep umber/charcoal masses, broad golden light, layered smoke and readable native animal silhouettes. Preserve the frightening progression of the accepted furnished-house replay. This is a fictional Catalogue Street composite, not a reconstruction of a surveyed Chicago address.

## Causal scene rules

1. **Closing routine.** A repeatable seed/input script may schedule ordinary shopkeeper and patrol routines. Begin with roughly 30–45 world seconds of quiet activity. The shopkeeper can leave the counter, use the real stairs and settle upstairs. Door actions, poses and carried props remain authoritative. Do not teleport an actor between routine waypoints.
2. **Lantern.** Taking the placed lantern removes that exact object/light from its support and attaches the same object to the player. It lights nearby ground and walls while moving. Reset restores one placed lantern, no duplicates. Keep placed/carried/spent lifecycle separate from the existing throwable-tool stock, as in the published interaction contract.
3. **One initial act.** The hero script uses actual movement, a reachable door/pickup and one match action on a measured combustible floor/stock site. Choose a site whose real path supports the intended progression. The command supplies finite heat to the facing target; all later ignition, smoke, light, failure, falling fuel and rubble come from shared state. No timed lists of new fires or house-wide destruction clock.
4. **Resident notices smoke.** A sustained, nonzero smoke/preheat signal at a real surface or plume location must be visible from the resident's current location through an actual opening. The resident looks toward that signal, approaches a safe viewing point if reachable and attempts the authored exit as conditions warrant. Use a documented, bounded perception approximation consistent with the shown smoke; this pass does not add a fluid/visibility solver. Art meshes, camera culling and the smoke display toggle cannot decide NPC perception. If the route is blocked, show a supported safe stop/alternative response rather than passing through geometry, fire or a missing floor.
5. **One officer, three states.** Preserve relaxed, suspicious and aggressive semantics. Seeing a carried lantern can form a memory; a nearby newly visible fire while that memory qualifies causes suspicion with the pistol stowed. Directly seeing a causal ignition can cause the existing draw/fire response, with live sight and cover checks. Seeing later spread is not direct evidence. Preserve the existing handling of unresolved delayed/mixed heat and projectile release/impact. The hero replay demonstrates concealed ignition and suspicion; the short control demonstrates direct evidence and cover.
6. **Scene-only memory default.** Allow a configurable 45-world-second carried-lantern memory in this isolated scene so an ordinary closing-time beat fits between sighting and fire. This is an architect-selected prototype value, not a new global rule. Keep the published default and its other evidence/draw/shot/expiry behavior unchanged. Record the actual sighting, expiry, origin distance and reason behind every transition. Do not force suspicion at a screenplay timestamp or turn general alarm into proof of guilt.
7. **Local damage and heat.** Reuse the furnished-house finite heat/fuel, boundary, opening, spill, support, falling-material and rubble rules. Preserve the 2–3 minute structural lifetime **per segment from its own ignition** at nominal burn, with real support loss recorded separately. Hot debris can ignite lower fuel; cooling rubble still supplies declining heat. Retire each local visible/collision/support patch together. Masonry can remain standing. Demonstrate a changed route only when actual geometry or an explicit thermal hazard makes that route unsafe.
8. **Known receiver limitation.** Milestone 21 diagnosed a selection sensitivity; it did not supply a production correction. Leave its no-lawn diagnostic hook disabled here. Freeze the complete physical composition before comparing/capturing it, including dressing that carries fuel, and record the receiver policy/domain. Do not use a secretly frozen old house selection or tune coefficients to force the narrative. A stable general receiver-policy correction remains a separate bounded proposal; report relevant limitations in this scene.
9. **Wind, light and clocks.** Use one declared deterministic wind sampler for heat/effects/cloth drift. Initial control is the existing 1.5 m/s east ambient sampler. The installed Ashland Nautilus bake belongs to its cottage obstacles and must not be relabelled as a Bell & Alder solution; a new obstacle-resolved bake is outside this first assignment. Keep this ambient limitation explicit. Use the same fixed camera/exposure before and after ignition. A burning room/building must visibly illuminate the street, opposite frontage and lower smoke beyond the carried lantern's reach. Preserve the bounded shared light budget and measure it; renderer units are not a calibrated lumen claim.
10. **Sound.** Establish a restrained street bed, actual footsteps upstairs, the pickup/door sounds, fire development and a small resident/officer reaction. The artist supplies the sound palette/event intent; the coder owns event-driven playback, limits, pause/reset/dispose and user-gesture audio startup. Reuse licensed installed material or simple procedural audio with documented sources. No new music or extensive dialogue production is needed. A mute option changes presentation only; include an audible capture when supported and report recording limitations.
