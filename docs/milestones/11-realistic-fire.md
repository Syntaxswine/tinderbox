## References and physical reading

Start from original repository/institution records and actual image files, not repost captions or an already-stylized fire atlas. Favor NARA, Library of Congress, US military/DVIDS and other authoritative public-domain records. A search thumbnail, US-government hosting, or a Commons upload by itself is not a rights determination. Record item ID, original URL and file URL, photographer/agency, date, original color versus later colorization, rights statement and its basis, access date, image dimensions, SHA-256, adaptations, and unresolved restrictions. Exclude images whose public-domain status cannot be established. Preserve originals under `art/source/fire-realism/`; runtime includes only deliberately selected self-contained derivatives and their provenance.

The source set must distinguish a liquid-fuel flamethrower jet, attached/pooled ground fire and structure fire with buoyant smoke. Historic color flame photographs establish shape, color, smoke and relative scale; they do not prove real-time turbulence or burn rates. Modern federal training photographs may supplement physical detail, clearly identified. Prefer non-graphic demonstrations/training and burning structures without visible casualties. User authorization covers finding suitable public-domain references; it does not require violent imagery or real-world ignition instructions.

Annotate the visible evidence: irregular connected flame fronts, bright cores with orange translucent edges, dark gaps and density variation, jet breakup and ignition lag, buoyant tongues/rolls, soot and smoke entrainment, convincing contact at the fuel, lighting spill and depth/occlusion. Compare the current four-frame crossed-card treatment directly with those features. Do not substitute uniformly opaque orange shapes, synchronized sine waves or repeating smoke stamps for observed complexity.

Use primary technical sources for real-time fire/volume rendering and performance methods. Label historical facts, inferred physical behavior, technical choices and game adaptations separately. No modern fire photo is an 1893 Exposition reconstruction.

## Quality-first baseline and shared effect API

First freeze the current published fire as the control. Build an intentionally detailed **higher-polygon reference** with genuinely useful silhouette/depth/temporal detail: subdivision/tongue shells, layered density, breakup and coherent motion, with smoke and bounded lighting. It must be a credible quality prototype, not a deliberately wasteful straw baseline. Record its actual geometry, shading, overdraw, lights, memory and measured costs. Approve its visual target before simplifying it. Preserve it as a reproducible review mode after the optimized mode becomes production default.

Artist export: `createFireRealism(THREE, {quality:'reference'|'optimized', ...options})` returns `{root, update(frame), reset(), dispose(), diagnostics()}`. Constructor receives the caller's shared Three instance, optional owned/shared texture handles and immutable settings; it must not fetch, create another renderer, modify simulation or read local absolute paths. `dispose` frees only owned resources. Coder may initially use an explicit unavailable/reference placeholder in a diagnostic, but cannot claim a placeholder is the artist's delivery.

`frame` is an adapter-owned detached v1 record: `{version:1, tick, worldTime, playerTime, worldDelta, playerDelta, paused, player:{x,y,elevation,radius,height,heading,tool,characterId}, camera, viewport:{width,height,pixelRatio}, anchors, cells, discharge, torch, events, eventReceipts, fieldUniforms, buildingVisuals}`. `cells`, `discharge`, `torch` and `events` retain their existing authoritative snapshot meanings/IDs; optional `buildingVisuals` supplies only legitimately burning building-group presentation, never invented gameplay ignition. Camera/viewport are presentation inputs. `fieldUniforms` refers to the existing authoritative fuel/burning mask and dimensions through explicitly borrowed textures; wrappers are detached, GPU textures are not cloned per frame. Coder documents any additive prepared data before the artist depends on it. The adapter does not replace resolved rays/fan/anchors with guessed reach.

Prepared `anchors` is `{muzzle:{x,y,z}|null, muzzleQuaternion:[x,y,z,w]|null, torchTip:{x,y,z}|null}` in the rendered world's metre coordinate system. Read the native attachment's actual world transform; missing anchors suppress attached emission and explain why in diagnostics. The authoritative discharge rays remain a separate ground-plane gameplay shape. `camera` is a detached `{kind:'orthographic'|'perspective', position:{x,y,z}, quaternion:[x,y,z,w], matrixWorld:number[16], matrixWorldInverse:number[16], projectionMatrix:number[16], near, far}` record. Matrix arrays use Three's column-major `toArray()` ordering; this is not another mutable Three camera. Pause sets both deltas to zero; rewind/generation changes reset effects and prime event receipts.

The following additive prepared fields are accepted for the corrective delivery:

- `opaqueDepth` is null or `{valid:true,texture,width,height,convention:'webgl-depth-0-1',viewProjectionMatrix:number[16],projectionMatrixInverse:number[16],cameraWorldMatrix:number[16]}`. The texture is borrowed, its dimensions are physical drawing-buffer pixels, and it represents the same camera and current frame. The artist terminates density integration at the sampled opaque intersection. The coder hides every fire root during capture, preserves actual visible/alpha-tested geometry, and excludes currently transparent, faded or `depthWrite:false` surfaces. In particular the existing 18% catalogue-house fade must not become opaque in this pass. Missing depth is diagnosed as unavailable occlusion, not accepted clipping. Every fire-specific prepass is inside measured fire cost and absent from the no-fire control.
- `buildingVisuals` is `[{id,prefabId,destroyed,burnedFraction,sourceBounds:{min:[x,y,z],max:[x,y,z]},footprintCellIds,burningCellIds,intensity}]`. Only an actually rendered housing group with burning cells in its existing `buildingId` collider footprint qualifies. Bounds come from the housing presentation. Facade/roof emission is a visual adaptation of that coarse burning group, not new upper-floor fuel simulation; nearby unrelated grass does not qualify.
- `eventReceipts` is `{generation,primed,highestId,events:[{...authoritativeEvent,receivedWorldTime}],reset:boolean}`. Authoritative numeric IDs survive the adapter. Initial entry primes without replaying historical events. Molotov/barrel bursts may use the explicitly labelled existing transient component while detailed ground/jet/torch replace their corresponding effects; do not duplicate either component or exclude its cost/resources.

The replacement adapter retains the legacy `diagnostics().discharge` object consumed by native held-tool visibility (`active`, `tool`, `connectRays`, `rayCount`, `muzzleInsideShape`, and relevant diagnostics). Artist-specific diagnostics remain nested rather than replacing that compatibility surface.

The architect accepts the coder's proposed UI driver surface: `FIRE_REVIEW_MODES = ['current','reference','optimized','no-fire']`, `FIRE_REVIEW_SCENARIOS = ['match-contact','carried-torch','gasoline-front','gasoline-front-powder','wall-clipped-jet','street-fire']`, and async `createFireReviewDriver({root,uiRoot,mountUI,onChange,signal,artistDelivery,referenceAcceptance})`. It returns `configure`, `play`, `pause`, `reset`, `step`, `seek`, `measure`, `cancelMeasurement`, `state` and `dispose`. `configure` uses `mode`, `scenario`, `view:'isometric'|'oblique'`, `lighting:'bright'|'dim'`, `population:null|32|128|512`, `seed`, `characterId` and `viewport`. Numeric populations are supported only in explicitly declared cases and report actual authoritative counts. State has nested `configuration`, `phase`, clocks, `availability`, `capabilities`, `workload`, `progress`, `referenceAcceptance`, `results` and `error`. It is detached and delivered through `onChange` outside timed sections. No missing artist mode silently falls back to current fire. Cost reports distinguish unavailable/cancelled/inconclusive/complete, retain raw trials, and cannot set target success without accepted quality and valid GPU evidence. The coder owns the complete implementation schema and tests; UI consumes it without implementing measurement rules.

Delivery metadata is mode-specific: `artistDelivery.byMode.reference` and `.optimized` each identify their own `sourceCommit`, factory, manifest URL and verified runtime-file hashes. The initial flat delivery is reference-only shorthand. Reference acceptance is an architect-authored `architect-fire-reference-acceptance-v1` receipt bound to the frozen reference closure. Optimized acceptance is a separate `architect-fire-optimized-acceptance-v1` receipt bound to its own immutable closure and that accepted reference revision. Maintain the user's mechanics: matches ignite one tile per press; a carried torch burns for a finite world-time duration; pouring follows accepted movement without gaps; the flamethrower's visible discharge agrees with obstruction-clipped authoritative rays/fan; powder slows fire/world motion while the animal can outrun a gasoline front. Pause freezes motion, stale events do not replay, reset/rewind/re-entry are deterministic, spent fuel stops emitting flames and finite smoke tails expire. Keep native animals/building dimensions, source geometry, collision, six controls and warm period HUD. No facade/roof is shown burning merely because unrelated grass is nearby.

Make the effect reusable without writing to the donor repositories: small renderer-injected factories, documented scale/clock/ownership and sample contract, immutable source provenance and bounded resource lifecycle. New effects still integrate and ship entirely inside Tinderbox.

## Review scenes and iteration loop

Use the same seeds, authoritative snapshots, active counts, lights, camera, exposure, output dimensions and render scale for current/reference/optimized modes. Keep one selected mode rendering at a time during performance measurement. Comparison UI and reference images are diagnostic tools and remain outside the public game package.

Required views are a single match/torch flame with visible fuel contact; a continuous moving gasoline front under normal and powder clocks; a flamethrower jet stopped by a real wall; and a multi-cell fire beside/on a legitimately burning street building group with smoke and retained animal/HUD readability. Include overhead isometric gameplay, a closer side/oblique view, bright and dim surroundings, camera movement, pause/resume, spent fuel and an occluder. Distinguish source/test fixtures from a played production scene. Building-fire rendering does not by itself add landmark fuel simulation or collapse physics.

Each iteration saves labelled, matching JPGs plus a short actual rendered motion capture or reproducible animated review. Keep before/after evidence and a short critique tied to specific references: silhouette/depth, temporal flow and breakup, color/opacity, smoke/light/contact, aliasing/pop/repetition, gameplay/HUD readability. Fix observed deficiencies and recapture. Artist and UI specialist provide independent critique; architect judges the combined runtime. Image metrics can flag lost silhouette/contrast, but do not establish realism or replace motion review.

The reference mode must pass that rubric before cost-reduction credit begins. Then simplify geometry, consolidate instances/materials, use profiling-led detail/LOD/culling, improve transparency fill/overdraw, and amortize work while preserving the approved view and motion. Record any tradeoff. Do not meet the target by dropping required smoke/lighting, reducing resolution, changing active fire population, hiding visible fire, changing clocks, replacing the scene, or increasing the reference cost after it is frozen.

## Measured cost and completion

Architect-selected initial test conditions are desktop 1280×800 and phone-shaped 390×844 at the product's documented pixel ratio; report actual hardware/backend, browser version and canvas size. A phone viewport is not a claim of phone hardware performance. Establish fixed small/medium/stress populations (initial suggestions 32/128/512 active cells) after the first baseline scene is validated, and freeze them before comparison.

Measure actual GPU elapsed time with `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` where supported, rejecting disjoint/invalid samples. Otherwise use a documented valid hardware profiling route and state precisely which costs it measures. Collect warm-up, multiple interleaved trials and enough frames for stable median/p95 estimates (initial default: 120 warm-up frames, 600 measured frames, three trials per mode). Separate fire-rendering time from the identical no-fire scene, and report absolute times plus ratios and variance; noisy or negative subtraction is inconclusive. CPU update/render submission, frame time, draw calls, triangles, overdraw/fill proxy, resources and leak-free exits accompany GPU timing. Fewer polygons or CPU-only measurements alone do not prove the requested rendering-cost target.

A single match or gasoline front can legitimately expire during that long sampling window. Therefore canonical cost collection may use a separately labelled **fixed authoritative-pose benchmark**: capture a real active simulation frame, freeze its fuel/cells/tool/anchors/masks and appearance inputs, and advance only presentation clocks identically for each compared mode, including the declared powder ratio. Record the capture frame, full source/workload fingerprints, actual active counts, per-frame occupancy and setup/resource ownership. Sustained emission is explicitly a benchmark adaptation, never evidence of played spread or burnout. Do not manufacture cells or resurrect events to maintain occupancy. Finite existing transients retain their declared clocks and their per-frame workload/cost is recorded. Compare exactly matching workload sequences; a mostly empty or mismatched canonical window is inconclusive. Pose capture/setup is outside steady-frame GPU cost, with reset/disposal and owned resource costs reported separately. Live deterministic scenarios remain mandatory for gameplay and motion acceptance.

The optimized version must retain the approved visual result and achieve **20–33% of the frozen reference's measured incremental fire-rendering cost** across the agreed representative cases. Faster than 20% is acceptable if quality is retained. Targeted workload fixes must not materially regress another required case.
