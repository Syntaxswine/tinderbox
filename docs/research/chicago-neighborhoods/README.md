# Chicago neighborhood sources and district plan

The owner requests a Hull-House neighborhood block next and wants Packingtown and further poor working-class neighborhoods included in Tinderbox. The first art brief is [the Hull-House block proposal](../../milestones/17-hull-house-block.md). This plan records the additional district intentions and their evidence needs.

## District sequence

| Area | Intended study | Source position and next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hull-House neighborhood | One block bounded by Halsted, Desplaines, Polk and Ewing, with street fronts and rear spaces | Selected on the inspected 1895 map. Trace original outlines, establish dimensions and match dated photographs for the first JPG preview. |
| Packingtown and the Back of the Yards | Workers' housing, local commerce and service routes beside the packing industry | Accepted district intention. Extend the existing stockyard research into the residential blocks. Select one mapped block using an 1890s atlas and dated housing views before the next asset brief. |
| Maxwell–Jefferson streets | An adjacent Near West Side study of small shops, tailoring and crowded housing | Use the original maps and 1895 book essays to locate a distinct area. Establish the period streetscape before using later photographs of the market. |
| Near North Side around the area called Little Hell | A further housing and industrial-edge study | Research candidate. Locate a compatible 1890s block plan and dated photographs. The identified 1902 photographic lead is a later comparison. |

Each district study should identify the actual kinds of housing, work and public spaces in its chosen block. Record residents' daily use and the mixture of maintained, repaired and deteriorated buildings where the sources show it. The owner's festive cloth skins can connect these settings to the fair as explicit game art direction.

## Packingtown and further neighborhood leads

The [1891–92 Newberry stockyard atlas](https://collections.newberry.org/archive/-2KXJ8Z814UYL2.html), [1890 Rascher panorama](https://www.loc.gov/item/98687207/) and [University of Chicago's period city map collection](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/collections/maps/chi1890/) anchor industrial geography. Select residential coverage and readable sheet scales for Packingtown. The [existing stockyard brief](../../milestones/16-stockyard-art.md) records the covered pen, gate and cloth-skin assignment.

The owner's [Stark Truth article](https://drloihjournal.blogspot.com/2021/09/the-stark-truth-about-chicago-in-1890s.html) supplies neighborhood and image leads. It labels its Bubbly Creek image 1915 and discusses Sinclair's 1906 book. Trace an image to its repository and date before placing its details in an 1893 reconstruction. Quantitative claims need their original records if used in game content.

For Maxwell–Jefferson, inspect the location and detail in the original maps and the contemporary book's housing and labor essays. Later market photographs should retain their dates and identified changes. The Chicago History Museum's [Near West Side lesson](https://www.chicagohistory.org/app/uploads/2020/05/Week6Teens-4.pdf) gives further archive leads, including market images dated 1933.

For the Near North Side, [this scholarly study](https://scholars.luc.edu/ws/portalfiles/portal/39986943/Chicago_s%20Little%20Sicily.pdf) identifies a Chicago Daily News photograph from September 22, 1902. Find the original Chicago History Museum item and an 1890s plan to establish a compatible block and streetscape.

The owner's supplied clock-tower image is captioned as the Pullman administration and factory complex. Keep that industrial reference under Pullman in later research, with the photograph's original collection and date to be established.

## Evidence for an asset handoff

Follow the [existing research method](../1893-exposition/README.md). Record repository, item identifier, image date, printed page or sheet, scale, calibration and uncertainty, plus the exact surfaces or dimensions supported. An imported image needs its item-level rights statement, source hash and adaptations. Keep the original reference files and annotations outside the public asset exports. Link the source facts and game dimensions in the artist's handoff so the architect can review both the JPG and its footprint.
